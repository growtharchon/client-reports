Two independent reasons this can't hurt the main site's rankings

First, every one of the 21 landing pages carries a noindex instruction, so none of them is eligible to appear in Google's organic results at all. Duplicate content is a problem that happens inside the search index — a page that can't enter the index can't compete with anything in it.

Second, even setting the noindex aside, the content genuinely isn't duplicated. Across all 21 pairs, textual overlap with the matching main-site page runs between 10% and 14%. Duplicate-content thresholds sit north of 80%. These are different pages written for different jobs.

Our recommendation is to leave the landing pages exactly as they are. Redirecting or taking them down would break the Google Ads campaigns currently running to them, which is a real cost incurred to solve a problem that doesn't exist.

02

The noindex is in place on all 21 pages — verified

We fetched each of the 21 URLs individually. All returned HTTP 200, and all 21 carry the noindex directive in the page head: 

<!-- present on all 21 landing pages -->
<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />
<link rel="canonical" href="https://theredstonemanufacturing.com/…" />

Verified 29 July 2026 · 21/21 pages · HTTP 200 · noindex present on every page.

The robots.txt file on the landing page domain is also configured correctly, and worth a specific note because it's a detail that is very commonly got wrong. Crawling is deliberately left allowed. That looks counterintuitive, but it's right: blocking crawlers would stop them fetching the pages, which means they would never see the noindex tag — and any URL that had already been indexed would linger there indefinitely. Allowing the crawl is what makes the noindex work.

What the 10–14% actually consists of

The overlap that does exist is the part that should overlap — and could not be removed without making one of the two pages wrong:

  • Certifications and facts: ISO 9001, IATF 16949, the Monterrey facility, USMCA tariff treatment.
  • Technical specifications: tolerances, axis counts, lead times.
  • Company identifiers: the Redstone name, contact details, industry terms like "CNC machining" and "die casting".

Two pages about the same business, describing the same certified facility, will always share this material. Google understands this perfectly well — it's why the threshold is set high, and why shared brand facts have never been treated as duplication.

05

One clarification worth putting on the record

"They're on different domains, so it's fine" is not the reason

This comes up often and it's worth being precise, because relying on it would leave you exposed on a future project. Sitting on a separate domain is not in itself a defence. Google detects and consolidates duplicate content across domains just as readily as within one, and genuinely mirrored content on a second domain can and does cause problems.

This case is safe for two specific, verifiable reasons: the pages are noindexed, and the content measurably isn't duplicated. Both were checked. The separate domain is incidental.

06

What we found that wasn't in the original question

Low priority · paid media

The landing pages are close copies of each other

Eric's question was whether the landing pages duplicate the main site. They don't. But while verifying that, we also compared the 21 landing pages against one another, and there the picture is different: within the CNC set, pages average 91% sequence similarity, with the closest pair — /custom-cnc-machining/ and /custom-machining-services/ — reaching 99.6%. The sheet metal set behaves similarly. These are essentially the same page with the target keyword swapped.

For organic search this is irrelevant — all 21 are noindexed, so none of them can compete with anything, including each other. It changes nothing about the answer above.

It is worth a look on the paid media side, where Google Ads assesses landing page experience and rewards pages that match their ad's specific intent. Someone searching "aluminum die casting" and someone searching "die casting companies in Mexico" want visibly different things. Differentiating the top third of each page — the headline, the opening paragraph, the proof points — is a modest change that tends to pay for itself in Quality Score and conversion rate.

We'd treat this as an optimisation to schedule, not a problem to fix. Happy to scope it separately.

Method and sources

Content comparison. Each landing page was matched to its closest equivalent on redstonemanufacturing.com and compared on two measures: sequence similarity over the full extracted body text, and Jaccard overlap of three-word phrase sets. Boilerplate was retained in both, which is the conservative choice — it inflates apparent similarity rather than hiding it, so the true content overlap is lower than the figures shown.

Technical verification. All 21 URLs were fetched live on 29 July 2026 and inspected for HTTP status, robots meta directive and canonical tag. The robots.txt file was reviewed directly.
